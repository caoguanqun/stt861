Overview

Course Description & Goals

STT 861 is a graduate-level introduction to the mathematical foundations of probability and statistical inference, designed for first-year master's students in Statistics. The course develops rigorous probability theory from first principles, beginning with set theory and the Kolmogorov axioms, and progressing through random variables, distribution functions, transformations, expectations, and moment generating functions. Common families of discrete and continuous distributions are studied in depth, followed by multivariate distributions, conditional expectation, and hierarchical models. The course concludes with the properties of random samples, order statistics, and fundamental limit theorems including the Law of Large Numbers, the Central Limit Theorem, and the Delta Method.

By the end of this course, students will be able to rigorously state and prove core theorems in probability theory, manipulate and derive the properties of standard univariate and multivariate distributions, apply transformation techniques to find the distribution of functions of random variables, compute expectations, variances, and moment generating functions and use them to characterize distributions, and reason carefully about convergence concepts that underpin large-sample statistical inference. More broadly, the course aims to develop the mathematical maturity and proof-writing skills that are essential for success in advanced courses in statistical theory, applied probability, and research.
